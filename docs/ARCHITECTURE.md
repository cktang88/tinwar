# Architecture

## Layout

- `src/shared/defs.ts` holds every tuning number: weapons, armor, perks, cooldowns and world constants.
- `src/shared/maps.ts` registers the maps and each mode's rotation; a map's `size` is its side in pixels. The versus maps live in `src/shared/maps/`, each drawn as a text grid of 50 px cells: the west half only, since `src/shared/mapgrid.ts` turns it half way round for the east and merges cells into wall, spawn and crate rects. Its legend is `.` floor, `#` concrete, `S` sandstone, `P` planter, `c` crate, `b` explosive barrel, `p` propane tank, `g` gas canister, `e` generator, `o` oil drum, `l` streetlamp, `m` medical cabinet, `a` ammo crate, `i` paint can, `R` red spawn (blue once turned), `F` FFA spawn, `X` both, and `A` zone A (B is the centre, C is A turned). Outpost, the zombies map, is still drawn in rects. `node scripts/map-lint.ts` checks every map for walls or crates past the edge, crates on walls, ground nobody can walk to, spawns a player cannot stand in, team spawns in sight of each other, a broken half turn and misplaced DOM zones, and lists each map's longest clear sightline against the longest gun's reach; `test/map-lint.test.ts` fails on any problem.
- `src/shared/protocol.ts` defines the wire messages and parses client input.
- `src/shared/wire.ts` encodes snapshots per connection: it rounds numbers and omits crates, leaderboard, zones and match while they are unchanged. The client rebuilds full snapshots from the last one it received.
- `src/shared/sim.ts` is the deterministic game simulation's entry point. `step` advances the world one tick, and the player commands add, remove, respawn and steer players. The rest lives in `src/shared/sim/`, one module per domain:
  - `world.ts` holds the world and player types, the seeded random source, map loading and spawn points.
  - `movement.ts` holds the collision geometry and player motion that the client also runs for prediction.
  - `stats.ts` holds perks, levels and score, and the effective stats they produce.
  - `combat.ts` holds bullets, damage, kills and lag compensation. Each input carries the server time of the world the client was drawing, and a shot first flies through that past, up to the client's measured round trip plus its render delay and never more than `MAX_REWIND_MS` back, so players hit what they aim at on screen.
  - `abilities.ts` holds the tier 3 abilities and the grenades, mines and gas they leave behind.
  - `dot.ts` holds the pulse clock every damage over time lands on (gas clouds, burning slicks, the Last Standing ring): one hit every 500 ms to whoever is in it then, the first 500 ms after the source starts, at the same damage a second, so a body shows one number and one hit sound a pulse. Spike strips in the horde mode still hurt every step, as they slow and wear by the step and show no numbers.
  - `barrels.ts` and `props.ts` hold the explosive barrels and the other shootable props (tanks, canisters, generators, drums, lamps, cabinets, paint): their health, what they do when shot to zero, the clouds, slicks and EMPs they leave, pickups and respawns. `propview.ts` is the footprint of a prop as the wire shows it, which the client predicts against.
  - `modes.ts` holds the FFA, TDM, DOM, zombies, Last Standing and shooting range rules and the round cycle.
  - `targets.ts` holds the shooting range: its targets, how every weapon hurts them, their regeneration, the readout and the loadout a range player may put on.
  - `royale.ts` holds Last Standing: the ring's phases, knocks and finishes, redeploys, supply drops, squads going out, the win, seats for joiners and whom the dead watch.
  - `downed.ts` holds the downed life both squad modes share: going down, the revive and bleeding out.
  - `run.ts` holds the zombies run: its day, night and restart cycle, the night table's hordes, survivors, ready-up, the win, reinforcements, walls and scrap.
  - `build.ts` holds the building rules, which the server applies and the client's build preview mirrors: what can go up where, prices and levels (a wall's tier, a turret's stats at a level, upgrade prices, what taking one down pays back) and what holding use tends. `utility.ts` holds what the utilities do beside the horde's tick: spike strips slowing, hurting and wearing, steel walls turning aside bites, the depot and the post.
  - `horde.ts` holds the zombies themselves: the flow field to the core, their steps, bites and spacing.
  - `snapshot.ts` builds each player's culled view of the world, centred on the player a dead Last Standing player watches.
- `src/server/` contains rooms, bots, accounts and the HTTP and WebSocket server. `bots.ts` runs each bot's think. `bot/` holds its parts:
  - `arena.ts` builds each world's nav grid (`nav.ts`, A* over a grid of where a body fits) and cover points (`cover.ts`, spots beside walls and crates with the bearings they shield) once per wall layout.
  - `awareness.ts` turns a bot's snapshot into what it knows: enemies in sight (in its `botSight` box, a person's 16:9 screen leaned toward its aim, see View), where it last saw others, hunted pings and Tracker, Recon, radar and Thermal marks on its minimap, and gunfire it heard (`heard`, from `heardShots` in `src/shared/sim/hearing.ts`: roughly placed, within the `BOT_HEARING` earshot). A bot shot at from off its screen turns to where the rounds come from (`unseenShooter`), as a person reads the tracers and the gunfire, and then answers that shooter first. A bot counts as on a human's screen (and thinks at the full pace) within the most that human's 16:9 screen could show at any aim: his view radius across and the screen's height, each widened by his gun's full lean (`screenReach` in `tick.ts`).
  - `intent.ts` holds the personalities, each weapon class's fight range, and the one transition table between intents (patrol, take position, engage, peek and hide, hold, reload in cover, retreat and heal, flank, search). An intent holds for a minimum time unless an interrupt fires.
  - `tactics.ts` is a bot's read of the fight, from nothing but what its screen shows (`docs/bots/GAP.md` lists what a good player does that bots did not, and where each is now handled). It remembers how it last saw each enemy (where, facing where, holding still or moving, reloading: every screen animates a reload), and with nobody in sight but someone about it pre-aims where he would come from, whatever it is doing: the edge of the cover he went behind, on his side of it, or the way a shot came (`watchPoint`); one who steps into that angle is taken in in 0.6 of its usual reaction time. It weighs each fight (`fightOdds`: the log of his time to kill it over its time to kill him, from both health bars as a share of full, both guns at that range, both armors, who has whom in front of his gun, reloads, a sprint's lowered gun, a slow gun that has just fired at it and is still cycling, its own suppression and post-sprint bloom, and every other enemy that can get on it against the mates by it), and takes it only above its temper's `takesOdds` (aggressive -0.45, marksman -0.2, cautious -0.05; a hunted enemy on odds 1 worse). Below that it breaks his line for cover hidden from every enemy it sees and holds the angle he would come from (`hold`, 2.5 to 4.5 s, then it goes round another way and takes what it finds); a fight in hand that sinks 0.3 under the line (a second enemy coming up on it, its health gone) is left at once, never a 1v2, unless the one it fights is nearly dead; one well in hand (odds over 0.6) is pushed rather than peeked, while one not clearly its own (under 0.3) seen coming, out of his fire, is met from cover with a lane on him and poked (and poked more often once in it), leaving that cover to push only at 0.9 (he is caught reloading, nearly dead or outnumbered). It shoots first whoever is shooting it, then the most dangerous, then the one it drops soonest (low health, reloading, aimed at it, near). From cover it pokes: shot at a quarter second into a peek it ducks back, a hide is cut short to catch a reload it saw start, a peek into an angle the enemy was last seen holding (planted, aimed within 15 degrees of it) is put off twice and then flanked, and a lost enemy last seen holding the way it would come is flanked or held rather than chased round his corner. With an enemy about it reloads only out of his line unless under 20% of its magazine. `node scripts/bench-freeze.ts` measures bots frozen facing away from every known threat, how often they face one, pokes, time outnumbered, deaths outnumbered, fights lost and life length.
  - `royale.ts` holds a Last Standing bot to its squad's anchor inside the ring, and has it revive and crawl.
  - `motor.ts` turns the intent into keys, aim and fire. No bot turns back within 400 ms of its last turn back. `aim.ts` holds the reaction and aim model: the gun turns toward where the bot wants it on a capped spring, the aim error drifts with a 400 ms time constant, and a bot fires only once its gun is on target. A bot takes in an enemy 220 to 350 ms after it comes into sight (`noticeMs`), scaled by its temper (`Personality.reactMul`: aggressive 0.8, marksman 0.85, cautious 0.95), and turns toward one caught well off its facing on a quicker hand (`HANDS.startle`) meanwhile. `BOT_AIM.errMul` (0.82) scales every bot's aim error: how steady its hand is. Its gun is a person's: the same spread, bloom and bloom recovery (there is no bot multiplier on any of them). A tapping gun's bot (`fireRhythm`, `holdsFire` in `motor.ts`) reads its own bloom off the rounds leaving its magazine and fires once the cone at the enemy's range fits him about as well as a settled gun would (`GOOD_SHOT`, 0.9 of the rested odds, a crossing enemy's lead counting against both), or once its patience since its last round runs out (the gun's pause times the temper's `commitMul`, at most 1.6 s: a bolt-action about 1.0 s for a hothead to 1.6 s for a marksman), and at once inside `TAP_FROM_PX`, when it is being shot or when the enemy is about to walk out of its sight. Assault-class guns keep their tap rhythm, the pause ending as soon as the next tap would land.
  - A room should read as a lobby of people, so bots come and go as people do (`src/server/lobby.ts`, `nextLobby` and `churn` in `room.ts`). When a match starts, 40 to 70% of the last match's bots stay for another round (those whose session is over leave first) with a fresh head for it; the rest leave and newcomers take their seats, never under a leaver's name. Each bot joins with a session length (a lognormal around 22 minutes, 6 to 60): mid-match, one whose session is over leaves at its next death (or a minute later), never within 2.5 minutes of the match's start or the room's last leave, nor in its last 30 s, and a newcomer takes the seat 3 to 8 s later. That is about one leave every few minutes in an 18-player room. Bots never come or go the tick a person does (`seatTick`, `seatsChanged`): a join, a leave or a friend crossing sides only moves the seat targets, a surplus bot leaving 2 to 10 s later (one at a time, one between lives first) and a missing one arriving 3 to 8 s later; only the room's real limit, its seats plus two spare, pushes a bot out at once. Now and then the head count drifts by one, a player arriving out of the blue or one leaving unreplaced. Zombies squads keep their bots for the run.
  - A bot is one player for its whole stay (`nextLoadout`, `respawnDelayMs`, `spawnSettleMs` and `pickDelayMs` in `bots.ts`, applied in `tick.ts`). It keeps the colour and armor it joined with, and its gun on about 85% of deaths; each life in a row ended without a kill past the first makes another gun 10% likelier (up to 55%). It clicks back in a lognormal while after it may respawn (median 1.8 s, a tail to about 8 s, and 2.5% of the time an AFK of 15 to 25 s), and a fresh spawn settles for 300 to 1200 ms, only turning to look round, before it presses a key; a hit or a round at it ends the settle at once. A versus bot takes an open perk, attachment or evolution 1 to 8 s after it opens (short waits commoner), 4 s later still while it is fighting. Each of these draws is hashed from the bot's name and the moment, so the room's random stream and a replay are untouched.
  - Nothing a client is sent says who is a bot. A snapshot's players carry `kind` only on the viewer's own entry (their bloom rules) and the board's rows no `human`: the server ranks the board itself, breaking a tie by a draw hashed from each player's id and the match (`boardTie` in snapshot.ts), never by who is a person, and sends Zombies the squad's horde share and how many people can ready (`share`, `crew` on the run view). `/api/servers` sends only how many are in each room, the friend menu treats a bot like anyone, and every name on the death recap and the board links to a service record: a name a room seated a bot under lately (the last 500, `noteBotSeen`) gets a stub (`src/server/botprofile.ts`), spelled as seated, its last seen when a room last had it and every other date hashed from the name; any other name 404s as an unknown one does. The stub has its level, matches, kills, deaths, medals and lifetime medals made up from its name alone, at a casual player's rates, the same every time. The level is the one on its name plate (`botLevel`), whose cosmetics come from what that level unlocks, and it wears its record's rarest lifetime medal. A bot renamed to make way for an account is dressed for its new name, and no bot takes a name with any stored profile. The server still knows (`Player.kind`, `RoomInfo.humans`) for moderation, logs and the bots' own brains, whose snapshots never leave it. Known tells left as game rules: in FFA only a person wins by reaching the kill target (a bot wins only on the timer, and a tie for the win goes to a person), easy mode halves a bot's damage to a person, and bots bloom twice what people do. Zombies squads are invite-only, so their run view still counts the people who can ready (`crew`) and lists who has (`ready`).
  - Bots are not all equally good. Each has a skill (`BOT_SKILL` in `aim.ts`), hashed from its name and the room's seed (`skillFor`), so the same name in the same room is the same player across its lives and each time it joins: half are rookies, a quarter regulars and a quarter veterans, with a continuous value inside each band. Skill is the brain only; a rookie carries a person's gun, body and health like any bot (`test/bot-parity.test.ts`). The rookie row has 2.6 times a veteran's aim error (`aimMul`; a regular's 1.35), takes enemies in 1.35 times as slowly, turns on a hand 0.7 times as quick, reads a target's motion three times as slowly, under-leads (0.8 of the lead, give or take 0.3), holds each tap 1.8 times as long and waits half as long on its bloom, reads a held angle a third of the time, takes fights 0.3 worse on the odds, flanks 0.4 times as often, notices someone come round its cover on a think only 35% of the time and lets half of its abilities' moments pass. The knobs run in a line from the rookie row (the rookie band's middle) to the regular row (the regular band's middle) to the veteran row, which is the identity: veterans are the bots as they were. On the aim range (`bench-bots.ts --aim-range`, one bot against a human who never fires back, 30 seeds) a rookie misses 1.16 to 1.45 times as many rounds as a veteran, 1.32 times on average over four ranges (48% against 33% at 250 px standing, 75% against 55% strafing; a regular 38% and 63%). In duels (`--duels`, the scripted human against one bot at a time, 30 seeds of 2 minutes) he beats rookies most easily and veterans least (today's figures are with fear, below). Name plates show no skill: a room should read as a lobby of people. The duel, flank, freeze, dodge, balance and zombie-curve benches play every bot as a veteran, so they measure what they did.
  - A green bot is a scared one (`fear` in `BOT_SKILL`: a rookie's 1, a regular's 0.3, a veteran's 0; `FEAR` in `aim.ts`), and its fear grows under pressure (rounds cracking past it, a hit in the last 1.5 s, health under half; `stressOf`, `fearNow`): a calm rookie carries 0.6 of its fear, a pinned one all of it. Fear is the root of its worst habits. It misjudges range, opening fire on an enemy out to 1.36 (calm) to 1.6 (pinned) times as far as a cool head would, past its gun's reach, so its rounds fall short, and reads its cone as fit as if he stood that much nearer; it sprays in a panic, holding each tap 1.3 to 1.5 times longer on top of its skill's and waiting 0.76 to 0.6 times as long on its bloom; it settles 1.25 times as far off as its gun's ideal, head-on and hold distances (a scared SMG hangs back at mid range); its odds of pushing a man shrink by half its fear; and shot at in cover it runs for it (0.8 times its fear) rather than repositioning. On the aim range a rookie's rounds leave in a 10.4 degree cone at 250 px standing against a veteran's 8.3 (8.7 before fear), it misses 44% against 29% (32% before), and with the man 900 px off 21% of its rounds are fired from past its reach (a veteran's none). In duels the human's K/D is 4.8 against rookies, 3.6 against regulars, 2.5 against veterans and 3.6 against the mixed draw (5.4, 3.7, 2.5 and 3.6 before fear), taking 541, 649, 707 and 648 damage a minute: a scared rookie keeps its distance, so it is hit less in an open field, and lands less. Veterans play exactly as before.
  - No two bots play alike, and none plays like a machine (`habits.ts`). Each has a movement style and habits hashed from its name like its skill (`styleFor`, kept on its memory across lives): its strafe legs (a base spread log-evenly over 725 to 2175 ms, from the old bots' mean leg up, with its own spread, a rookie's longer and a veteran's more varied, never under 450 ms), how often it pauses a beat between legs (a rookie more), leg angles that wander a few degrees off the 45/90/135 marks, a lean toward closing in or backing off, its odds (0.05 to 0.5) of running a second leg the same way so it drifts rather than rocks, how much open ground it wants ahead of a leg (120 to 180 px), a planted gun's sidestep odds at or under its temper's; variety only ever makes a bot easier to hit, never harder, than the bots of old; its share of sprinting when it could, the share of its magazine it reloads at (0.3 to 0.95), how often a rookie forgets and runs dry, whether it shoots crates for score (some never, some always, after a look of 0.3 to 1.5 s), how often it idles, wanders off, grabs a pack mid-fight, fixes on one man, goes after its killer and is drawn by gunfire. Getting where it wandered or searched to, it may stand and look round for 1 to 6 s, drift a few steps, or, rarely and only with nothing about, sit with its hands off the keys for 2 to 8 s; shot at or hit, it breaks off at once and goes to where the rounds came from. Gunfire at the bot itself always draws it; other gunfire draws an idle bot at 0.8 within 600 px falling to 0.15 at 2500 px, by temper, half that for one busy farming or holding a spot, one decision per stretch of map for 8 s. It remembers who killed it (read off the kill feed while it lies dead, `diedTo`) and goes looking where he was for half a minute or so; in a fight it may stay on one man past where it should break off (more on its killer, more when tilted). After a kill it stands and looks round for 200 to 800 ms and reloads on reflex at 0.5 to 0.8 by its habit. Its reaction is lognormal (`NOTICE` in `aim.ts`: a median of 295 ms times its sharpness, temper and mood, log-sd 0.3, never under the old flat draw's quickest 220 ms times the same, so no quicker on average than before, up to 90 ms more for a man behind it, 1.5 times as long when it is fighting another it can still see, 100 to 200 ms more when one turns up within 220 px out of nowhere); busy fighting or reloading, it overlooks a man at the edge of its screen on a think at 0.3 (veteran) to 0.6 (rookie); and it never tracks a man through a wall or a crate: out of sight it aims where he was going (his motion fading over 300 ms) and lets go of the trigger once 250 ms of spray has carried on (`motorTick`). Its moods only ever make it worse: warming up (its first 30 to 60 s in a room) it aims and reacts up to 15% worse; two or three quick deaths (lives under 20 s) inside a minute tilt it (looser fights, more tunnel vision, aim 10% worse, fading over 45 s); late in a long stay (5 to 10 min) it gets bored and idles and wanders more; a veteran feels half of each; a new map starts its moods and grudges afresh. Gun, body and health stay a person's (`test/bot-parity.test.ts`).
  - A bot never sits still behind cover while it is being shot (`answerFireInCover` in `intent.ts`). Its cover hides all of it: a cover spot counts only when the bot's whole body, its radius and 3 px round it, is out of every threat's line, judged from where he stands by the line to its centre and to points across it out to its edge (`bodyExposed` in `cover.ts`), at the spot and a step short of it, where the motor pulls up; a spot whose shoulder shows past the corner, or a lone 44 px crate (narrower than a 48 px body), is no cover, and cover an enemy in sight can see any of it at is blown. A cover point whose nav cell the wall's clearance closes stands a little further out rather than being dropped (which had left only the corners). Hit at its spot all the same (250 ms or more after it got there), it tucks into the nearest spot that hides all of it from the shooter; pinned there by rounds cracking past (0.9 to 1.4 s, by lane) it does something by its temper: pushes a man caught reloading or on worse odds (its temper's `pushOdds`), peeks him from another bit of cover (`peekOdds` + 0.3), goes round him (`flankOdds`, + 0.3 when outgunned), or shifts to other cover, and with nothing else to hand it flanks. A scared bot runs instead. Where the fire comes from is what it can tell: the one it sees shooting it, where a round fired its way came from, the man who hit it as it last saw him. The new intent's own clock is its hysteresis, so it reacts again only once settled at the next spot and never shuttles between two.
  - With nobody to shoot, a bot's gun is never dead still (`swayAt`, `SWAY` in `motor.ts`): a slow sweep and a smaller, quicker wander, three sines at the bot's own frequencies (0.08 to 0.85 Hz) and phases hashed from its id, so a replay is exact and the random stream untouched. Holding an angle or a spot it wobbles 3.5 degrees either way, walking it looks 9 degrees round the way it goes, and standing with nothing to look at it looks round (`scanPoint`): at a sound it heard, else at the lanes open from where it stands within a quarter turn and a bit of its aim, a new one every 2.5 to 5.5 s, turned to on a slow hand (`HANDS.scan`, at most 70 degrees a second, so it never snaps). The sway peaks near 30 degrees a second, slower than a person sweeps his screen, so the lean of its sight box toward its aim (`botSight`) shows it no more than a person looking round would. An enemy in sight, a startled turn and a throw carry no sway: once it takes a man in, the aim model has it unchanged.
  - A bot in cover re-checks it on every think against each enemy it can see (`coverBlown` in `intent.ts`): an enemy with a line into its hiding spot sends it to cover from him, a rusher in reach at him, or into a fight from where it stands. `tick.ts` wakes a bot the tick an enemy in its view box gets a line on it, and a bot answers the enemy hitting it or firing its way before the one it was fighting. `node scripts/bench-flank.ts` measures how fast a bot faces, fires back and moves when a human comes round its cover.
  - Bots have a social side (`social.ts`), run by the room once a tick after `thinkBots` and never by the scripts that drive `thinkBots` alone (the golden replay and the benches are unchanged). Everything is hashed from the bot's name and rolled on its own generator, on the world's clock. **Chat**: one bot in five is chatty (one in ten of those twice as chatty), the rest almost never type; a chatty bot rolls a line at a death (12%), a second death to the same man (25%), a kill (5%), a multi-kill or the top player's head (15%), a round's end (25%), a "gg" echoed at a round's end (25%), being named (35%), a question (10%; a question or a line at it gets only "?", "idk", "what", "lol" or "huh", nothing it could be held to), "are u a bot" (30%, a non-committal "?", "bro what", "huh" or "lmao": a bot may stay quiet or deflect but never denies it or claims to be human), "?" or "nice shot" from a man it just killed, "ez" from the one who killed it, and now and then to nobody. Lines come from a phrase bank of short lowercase chat by temper (an aggressive bot trash-talks, a cautious one is politer), in the bot's one style (its laugh, "u" or "you", a caps habit, one in seven chatty bots in Portuguese, Spanish or Indonesian), with the odd typo, never one said in the lobby in the last 5 minutes; they wait a typing delay (0.6 to 1.5 s plus a second per 3.5 characters, replies 2 to 8 s), mostly for a calm moment, and are dropped 10 s after what they answer. Caps: a line per bot per 20 s, 4 a minute, 15 in ten minutes, and one bot line in the lobby per 6 s; a follow-up (the second line of a burst, 20%) comes 1.5 to 4 s after the first and still counts toward the minute and ten minutes. Every line, from the bank or a plugged-in writer (`LineSource`, which may answer with a promise), passes `cleanLine`: the room's chat filter, a blocklist (self-harm, sexual, political, identity, links, handles, personal details, digits but "1v1"), no word of bots, models, prompts or being human, spaced, dotted or run together, self-harm however spelt, 40 characters, lowercase; the text is folded with NFKC and look-alike letters first, and a line with any other letter left is refused. Lines and emotes go out the way people's do (`sendChat`, `sendEmote` in `room.ts`), under the same chat and emote limits. **Emotes**: now and then a taunt, a laugh, a thumbs-up or a "gg" at a kill or a round's end. **Goofs**, rare and only while nothing threatens the bot (`threatened`: no enemy near or in its screen with a line, no hit in 3 s; one ends the moment that changes): a spin of a full turn or more, a quick wiggle of the keys, two or three rounds fired where nobody (and no barrel, prop or crate) stands, stopped the moment anyone steps into the line or any enemy, a truce partner too, is near, and a goofy bot's burst of emotes, played as a short override of the input the motor chose, with keys and trigger let go the tick it ends. **Truces** (FFA only: in a team mode a man at peace with one bot could shoot its teammates): a person who spins (a full turn of his aim within 1.2 s, `watchSpin`) or waves, salutes or thumbs-ups at a bot within 800 px in its sight without having fired for 2.5 s or hit it for 6 s may get a spin or a wave back and a truce of 25 to 60 s (one sign makes peace with two bots at most, and a bot takes no new truce from a man for 2.5 minutes after one ends or it refuses), likelier from a chatty or cautious bot; a calm chatty bot now and then offers one to a person nearby who is not fighting (a wave or spin, maybe "team?"), held 7 s for an answer. A truce is the one hook in target choice, `socialHoldsFire` (`spareTruce` in `botThink` drops him from its threats); his shot ends it at once, and an aggressive bot betrays 40% of its truces (a marksman 18%, a cautious one 7%). Zombies squad bots only now and then say "reloading", "nice" or "behind u". `node scripts/social-meter.ts [minutes] [seed]` plays a seeded 16-bot FFA room with two scripted people: over 10 minutes, seeds 1 and 2 gave 0.7 and 0.8 lines and 0.8 and 0.2 emotes a minute, 81% and 88% of bots never typed, and 10 and 17 goofs.
- `src/client/` contains the browser client.
  - The menu's front page: the top bar (Log in and Register, or your name and level bar linking to your own service record, Account and Log out) and the bottom bar (What's new, Discord, feedback, privacy) are `menubar.ts` and `public/menubars.css`; the mode dropdown is `modepicker.ts`.
  - **What's new** is `src/client/changelog.ts`. When a change ships that players will notice, add a short plain line at the top of the newest day (or a new day above it), and keep the list to about 20 lines by dropping the oldest.
  - `src/client/config.ts` holds `DISCORD_URL`, the community invite; the bottom bar's Discord link stays hidden while it is empty. Feedback links (menu, death card, pause menu) mail `halberd8@gmail.com` with the mode, map, screen and browser filled in (`contact.ts`).


## Performance

`node scripts/bench-tick.ts [mode] [maps] [humans] [players] [seconds]` runs a full room in one process the way `room.ts` ticks it and prints ms per tick split into bot think, step, snapshot build and encode, with snapshot bytes per player. The October 2026 pass, 1 person and 17 bots on airbase, wasteland and embassy (4-core box, other loads running, so medians):

- **Step**: about 1.1 to 0.7 ms a tick. Swing doors read their shut leaves from a cache (built once per door, with a box to skip far bodies), and who is alive or walking is read once for all doors. `coverRects` and `solidRects` are kept per world and handed out again while walls, crates, barrels, props and buildings are unchanged, rather than a fresh 150 to 700-wall copy for every player's move. `w.walls` keeps its array while no wall expires. A rewound shot uses the walls array its history frames share, rather than a deduplicated copy.
- **Encode**: about 0.13 to 0.08 ms per player a tick. Numbers are rounded into a copy that plain `JSON.stringify` writes (a replacer function is three times slower). Sticky fields are compared with what was last sent in place, rather than stringified every tick. The output is the same to the byte (`test/wire-encoder.test.ts`).
- **Whole tick**: median about 2.0 to 1.5 ms with 1 person (about 6% to 4.5% of a core), and 3.0 to 2.0 ms with 8. A room with no humans does not tick (4 empty rooms are under 1% of a core in all).
- **Bytes**: about 1.1 KB a snapshot, 33-40 KB/s per player. `SKIRMISH_DEFLATE=1` turns on permessage-deflate, which brings that to 4-6 KB/s for about 1% of a core per player. It is off by default: when the event loop is saturated (the e2e test's 8x speed), sockets waiting on zlib build a backlog, and the stall rule cuts them off. `measure-bandwidth.ts` prints the bytes that cross the socket.
- **Client**: `PROFILE=<file> DPR=3 node .claude/skills/verify/scripts/frametime.ts "$RUN" 15 852 393` also records a CPU profile and logs the main thread's top self time and GC pauses. In headless Chrome without a GPU, most frame time is raster. In JS, the CPU fallback for the night light (`drawNightFx`, whose two full-view composites stand in for the WebGL lighting pass) and blurred loose shadows lead. Snapshot decode is about 0.1%, and GC runs 1 to 2.5 short pauses a second. `matchMedia` queries made several times a frame (about 3.4 ms/s) are now made once.
