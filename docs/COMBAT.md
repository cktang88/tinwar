# Combat

A round leaves the muzzle at five times its cruise speed and eases down over its first couple of hundred px, so up close it lands before anyone can step aside, even a target already strafing, while at range most rounds fly slowly enough to see coming and sidestep; sniper rounds cruise fast but get a gentler kick out of the muzzle, so a sniper does not also own close range. How a gun handles comes from what it is (see Handling below): pistols and SMGs fire as true on the run as standing still, a long, heavy gun sways on the move (an assault rifle about 1.7 times its still spread, a machine gun about 2 times, a shotgun a little), and a planted sniper is pinpoint down to a small floor (see Weapon roles below). No gun is ever a laser: each has a spread floor it never fires under, however planted, steady or perked (bigger rounds and shorter barrels group worse: about 1.3 degrees either side of the aim for a pistol, 0.7 for an SMG, 0.6 for the long guns; a set-down bipod's own is 0.7), so the reticle never closes to a point. Holding the trigger fires any gun at its top rate: an automatic as ever, a semi-auto as fast as it cycles (as fast as the quickest clicking could), a burst gun burst after burst. Every gun blooms when fired fast: from the second round of a spray (a pistol's sixth, a burst gun's round after its burst, the semi-auto snipers' and shotguns' third) each round kicks the spread wider by what its calibre kicks against the gun's weight and length, the first kicks hardest and each later one less as the cone nears its cap (see The bloom curve below), and it cools once you pause, so paced taps stay tight. On the move a gun blooms the full way; standing it grows only part as much (a light gun more, a heavy one less: 70% for a pistol, about half for a rifle, 40% for an LMG) and with a bipod set down 30%, always on top of its floor. Every mode handles guns the same way: bloom, moving spread, suppression, muzzle kick and recoil apply in Zombies and Last Standing just as in FFA, TDM and DOM. Each round is drawn as a lit slug with a hot white-yellow core, an amber body and a warm tracer, fatter and with a longer streak the harder the gun hits, glowing at night. A round that lands knocks its victim back along the shot, in proportion to the damage and the gun's class: about 15 px/s from a pistol round, 5 from an SMG's, a point-blank shotgun blast the 120 px/s cap, a bolt-action round about 95, and blasts up to 180 px/s. The shove bleeds off in about a quarter second, slides through cover like any step so nobody is pushed into a wall, and heavy armor lets only 70% of it through; zombies take it by weight (runners most, brutes and the colossus not at all). The server owns it and sends your own shove in your snapshot so your movement prediction replays it, and a victim visibly flinches, flashes and sprays more the bigger the hit. Muzzle flashes grow with the gun, with a brief floor light pool and, for shotguns and snipers, a big bloom. Bots play by the same rules: they aim where the round will meet a moving target, found from the target's velocity and the round's flight along the muzzle curve, with a slightly different judgment of the lead each time they engage, their aim widens when suppressed, and rounds cracking past make them sidestep and duck like a hit would. An enemy round passing within 60 px of you suppresses you: your spread widens by up to 25%, a planted sniper loses its pinpoint aim, the edges of your screen close in under a heavy dark vignette that hides them almost entirely at full suppression, and the HUD switches to its high-contrast colors so you can still read your ammo. Each round adds what its gun suppresses, and suppression holds only 0.15 seconds before it fades, so only sustained automatic fire keeps it building: an LMG pins you down in under a second and its shadow lingers for about a second and a half after it stops, an assault rifle or SMG does so in one to two seconds, while a pistol barely builds any and a sniper's crack is shaken off in about half a second. What you carry slows you down: every kilogram of gun and armor (and a little for a long gun) takes the same share off your walk and more off your sprint (light, medium and heavy armor weigh 3, 6 and 9 kg, about 10%, 19% and 29% of your speed; a machine gun costs about a quarter), but no loadout drops below 58% of base speed; the Lightweight attachment gives 25% back. A grenade, mine or other blast shakes your screen, harder the closer and bigger it is, and big guns (shotguns, rifles, hand cannons, machine guns) kick the camera back along your aim when you fire. A fallen player lies where they died for 30 seconds, a grey body crossed out with an X, knocked back along the killing shot, in a pool of blood sprayed out the far side, with their gun slid to a random spot and angle beside them; an explosive death leaves a darker, scorched body on soot with the gun flung further. Corpses fade out over the last 3 seconds, and a new map clears the field. In Zombies every zombie the squad kills stays where it fell until the night ends, eyes crossed out and limbs splayed in a pool of ichor sprayed away from whoever killed it; a pile of them merges into one outlined heap, and the whole field fades at dawn.

**Health.** A person and a bot carry the same health and regen, and every gun and body stat is the same for both but bloom (speed, spread, reload, cooldowns, pickups, perks: `test/bot-parity.test.ts` runs a bot and a person through the same inputs and checks the sim treats them alike). A person blooms half what a bot does (see People bloom less than bots, below). Bots get no handicap either: person on person, person on bot, bot on person and bot on bot all hit for the gun's own damage. Armor blocks a flat share of each hit: 8%, 16% or 24%.

Points in one life raise your level. Damage is the score: every point of health you take off an enemy pays a point, so a kill from full health is worth 100 and 37 damage on one enemy plus 63 on another counts the same; every point of damage you take from an enemy and live through pays a point too, and every 30 seconds alive pays 33 (a third of a kill). There is no flat kill or assist pay (an assist is still named in the kill feed). Medals pay on top, at 60% of what they once did; with them and the catch-up bonus a typical kill comes to about 185 points (measured in rooms of bots; `test/balance.test.ts` holds it). At 240 points, around your first or second kill, you pick an attachment. At 500, around the second or third kill, your gun evolves, and at 1210, around the sixth or seventh, it evolves again, each time into one of two branches: 6 class guns, 12 stage-1 guns and 24 stage-2 guns. Between the two evolutions, at 770 and 990 points (around kills four and five), you pick a tier-2 perk (four drawn at random for each life from fifteen) and an ability, so a pick comes about every kill. On a desktop the top left shows what the life has picked (`src/client/loadout.ts`): one plain line with your level and what comes next ("LV 2 · Next: Perk · 560 / 770", "Pick a perk" while one waits, or the score once the ladder is done), and under it a tile for each pick: the evolved gun, the attachment, the perk and the ability, each with its icon and short name. Nothing shows for a pick not yet earned; each tile stamps in as its pick lands, hovering one with the cursor says what it does, and the ability's tile sweeps through its cooldown with the seconds left and turns orange with a glow and its key when it is ready. A gun evolve is always offered before any perk you have not picked yet, so an ignored perk never holds back a new gun. Dying resets your score, perks and gun to the class gun. While your level is below the average level of the other living players, every point you earn counts 1.5 times. A player holding a stage-2 gun is hunted: every enemy minimap pings their position every 2.5 seconds and whenever they fire an unsilenced shot (not with Ninja: then only the 2.5-second ping finds them), the kill feed announces it, and killing them pays a 150-point bounty on top of the kill. An attacker who dealt at least 30% of a victim's max health earns a 50-point assist when someone else gets the kill. A player killed by their own blast gives the kill to whoever hurt them most in the last 10 seconds.

**Sniper tempo.** A sniper is a commitment, not a spam gun. The bolt-actions (Bolt-action 1.75 s between shots, Longshot and Piercer 1.92 s, Artillery 1.7 s) work the bolt by hand after every round: the soldier's support hand leaves the fore-end, lifts the handle, draws it back, runs it home and locks it over exactly the fire interval (`boltScene` in `src/client/reloadanim.ts`, on the `BOLT` beats in `src/client/reloadbeats.ts` that the bolt foley also plays on), and your reticle stays grey, faint and dotless at its true gap until the next round is chambered, then flashes bright; a click while the bolt is still well short of home plays the "not yet" tick and shakes it. Each round also kicks the cone open, even planted: a pinpoint gun's bloom is a kick of its own still spread on top of its floor, so straight after a shot a bolt-action's cone opens to about 3.4 degrees either side of the aim (6.5 times its floor), and it takes about 2.4 s to close back to the floor. The fastest follow-up, ready at 1.75 s, still flies about 4.5 times as wide as a patient one (about 2.4 degrees), and firing every round as fast as the bolt allows holds it at the widest; you see it on the reticle, which opens with each shot and visibly tightens back. The semi-autos (Semi-auto Rifle and Ghost 0.39 s, Repeater 0.25 s) kick less and settle sooner: two quick rounds fly clean, each further quick one widens the cone (about 1.8 then 3 degrees for the Semi-auto), and it is gone about 1.2 s after the last. One shot from full health drops: the Bolt-action, only the unarmored (two rounds drop any armor); the Longshot and Artillery, up to light armor; the Piercer, up to medium; nothing drops heavy armor in one except an Artillery shell with the Demolitions perk; the semi-autos always take two or more. Bots fire their snipers the same way: a bot lets the bloom settle before its next long shot (`tapRhythm` reads the gun's bloom).

What the rules add beyond the old stat sheet (`GunRules` in `src/shared/defs.ts`): `falloff` (a round keeps full damage out to `startPx`, then fades to `minMul` by `endPx`: SMGs, shotguns, the Machine Pistol, Akimbo, Gunslinger), `deploy` (stand still for `ms` and the spread is multiplied by `spreadMul`: the bipod LMGs), `shoveMul` (knockback per hit) and `breach` (a round that strikes a swing door blows it open). The rest of a gun's handling is not written per gun but worked out from its build (see Handling): its sway on the move (`movingSpreadAdd`), its spread floor (`minSpread`; a bipod's `deploy.minSpread` replaces it while set down), its bloom's kick, cap, recovery and standing share (`bloom.still`; `deploy.bloom` with a bipod down), its post-sprint `settle`, and how fast it lets you walk and sprint. Bots fight each gun from the distance its job wants (`GUN_BAND` in `src/server/bot/intent.ts`): rushers close in and never back off, a plant-to-aim or bipod gun holds its lane, and from 280 px out a bot whose gun blooms with its rounds (an assault rifle, a sniper, an SMG or auto pistol that holds a lane) fires in taps of its gun's `tap` rounds at most, holding each next round until its cone at that range fits the target about as well as at rest, while rushers and machine guns hose, a machine gun because a stream pins (`tapRhythm`).

**Handling.** Every gun and evolution has a weight, a length and a calibre (`kg`, `cm`, `calibre` in `src/shared/defs.ts`; each calibre's muzzle energy is in `CALIBRES`), and every armor tier a weight; a few documented formulas in `src/shared/handling.ts` work out the rest, so a gun handles like what it is instead of from a hand-set number. A bigger round kicks harder and groups a hair worse; a heavier gun soaks more of the kick but sways more on the move and takes longer to bring back on target; a longer gun is steadier and groups tighter but swings more (its moment of inertia, weight times length squared, sets the walking sway and the post-sprint settle). Each round's bloom kick is its energy (both rounds of a paired pull) over the gun's weight and length, grown by the square root of its cadence past an assault rifle's 9 rounds a second (faster than that, the muzzle is never back on target before the next round), up to a cap from the round and the weight; `cap / kick` is how many rounds a held trigger takes to reach the cap. A light, short gun is held close and its muzzle cannot wander far, so its whole bloom (kick and cap alike, so it takes the same rounds to its cap) is held to its reach, `0.6 + 0.4 x I^2 / (I^2 + 1.5^2)` of the build's, I being its moment of inertia (`HANDLING.capReach`): a pistol 0.6, an SMG 0.67, a Skirmisher 0.62, an assault rifle 0.94, a machine gun about 1, so spraying an LMG stays punished. A shotgun's bloom is held to 0.65 of its build at most (`HANDLING.shotgunCapMul`): its pellet pattern is its spread. Full auto is punished standing as well as moving: standing grows 0.6 (the heaviest MG) to 0.95 (a pistol or SMG) of the moving bloom, 0.85 for an assault rifle, so planting your feet helps only a little.

**The bloom curve.** Shooters model spray bloom a few ways. Battlefield adds a fixed spread increase per shot (SIPS) up to a max spread and takes it back at a fixed rate a second: a straight line that hits its cap like a wall. Counter-Strike adds a fixed inaccuracy a shot and decays it exponentially (a recovery time to a tenth), which under held fire is a leaky integrator that creeps toward a plateau set by the fire rate, and whose tail never quite clears between taps. Valorant gives the first shot full accuracy and ramps error over the next few rounds to a plateau. The usual diminishing-returns forms are the exponential approach (`b += (cap - b) k`: never reaches the cap, one fixed shape), tanh (`cap tanh(n / n0)`: the same, asymptotic) and a per-shot kick scaled by how much room is left, `kick (1 - b/cap)^p`. Tinwar uses the last, with 0 < p < 1, because its exact solution is a closed form in the rounds fired:

`share(n) = 1 - (1 - n/N)^q` for n < N, and 1 from N on, with q = 1/(1 - p),

where n is the rounds of the spray past its first (`free`: 1 for a full-auto gun, a burst gun's whole burst and one more (its first burst flies on its cone, and held, the bursts after it bloom), 2 for the semi-auto rifles and shotguns, 3 for the Marksman, 5 for a pistol, so a sidearm's first five rounds stay on its cone; a semi-auto or burst gun keeps its heat until it could fire again (`coolsAfter` in `trigger.ts`), so fired as fast as it goes it blooms, while paced taps start cold), N = `cap / kick` is the rounds to the cap and q is the curve's `shape`. The round after the free ones adds q times the mean kick and each later one less, as `(1 - b/cap)^(1 - 1/q)`, so a held trigger bites at once and slows as it nears the cap, landing on it at N rounds with zero slope and never past it. Unlike the asymptotic curves it keeps N meaningful (it is still the rounds a spray takes to cap), q = 1 is exactly the old straight line, and a large q tends to the exponential approach, so one shape knob spans them. The shape comes from the gun's weight, `q = clamp(2 / (kg / 3.6)^0.45, 1.3, 3.4)` (`HANDLING.shape`): a light gun (a pistol 3.4, an SMG 2.4) snaps up and flattens, a heavy one (an LMG 1.3, the Minigun 1.3) rises slower but carries its momentum nearly straight to the cap, the assault rifle sits at 2. The sim keeps one number, the spray's heat in rounds; `bloomShare` and `coolSpray` in `src/shared/sim/trigger.ts` are the only implementation, used by the sim, the page's prediction (`src/client/fire.ts`) and a bot's read of its own cone (`ownBloom` in `src/server/bot/motor.ts`). Recovery is linear in that heat once no round has left for `settleMs`, so the cone comes back down the curve it climbed: slowly off the cap, then quickly near rest. A full-auto class's `settleMs` sits just past the longest gap between held rounds of any of its guns (105 ms for the SMGs, 140 for the assault rifles and the machine guns), so a held trigger never cools between rounds and a let-go one starts cooling at once. A full spray's heat then clears in the gun's `recoverMs`, `123 x (kg / 3.6)^0.6 x (cm / 90)^0.7` (`HANDLING.recover`; length weighs more than weight, as in the gun's moment of inertia): about 60 ms for an SMG, 40 for a Skirmisher, 120 for an assault rifle, 245 for an LMG and 320 for the Minigun, so a small gun is brought back twice as fast as under the old `190 x (kg / 3.6)^0.5 x (cm / 90)^0.3` and a big one about 1.3 times. A scoped gun keeps that old formula times 6.7 (`HANDLING.scopeRecover`, then 1.6 more for a bolt-action), so a sniper's bloom still takes its seconds to settle. The reticle (and the cone a shot gets) eases bloom back out at the gun's own pace too: over its `recoverMs`, kept between 100 and 250 ms (`spreadEaseDownMs`; see Sprint below), so a capped spray is back within a tenth of its rest in about 0.23 s for an SMG, 0.2 s for a Skirmisher, 0.3 s for an assault rifle, 0.57 s for the Minigun and 0.6 s for an LMG (it was 0.37, 0.37, 0.43, 0.57 and 0.6 while every gun's bloom eased out over the full 250 ms): an SMG's reticle settles in under 0.4 of an LMG's time. That is what makes bursting pay at range: standing at 450 px, four rounds and a 150 ms let-go land about 7.4 hits a second from an assault rifle against 5.5 held, and 6.3 from an LMG against 6.3 held, and up close (150 px) a held trigger wins with every gun. An SMG is not made to burst at 450 px: it is past its falloff there (its rounds do 30%), its cone at rest (6.3 degrees either side) is twice a body's width, and its bloom is now small and gone so soon that a held trigger loses little (5.1 hits a second held, 5.0 from four rounds and a quick 100 ms let-go). Its rest cone is left as it is: tightening it would make it a mid-range gun, which is the assault rifle's job. A short tap sits low on the curve where it is steep and clears in a blink (a tap every half second is back on the first round's cone every time, four a second stays within 15 per cent, the kick easing out over `SPREAD_EASE`), while a long spray has banked heat on the plateau and holds its width a while; the Counter-Strike way round (fast first, slow tail) would leave a residue on every tap and reward spraying. An assault rifle held down is 1.6 times as wide by round 3, 3.1 times by round 10 and caps at 3.9 near round 19; an SMG 1.2, 1.5 and 1.6 (its cap a third lower than its build alone would give: 3.8 degrees of bloom, from 5.6); an LMG 1.2, 1.8 and 2.4 by round 30. Burst guns fly each burst on its first round's cone and cool between bursts. `tap` (written per class: three for an assault rifle, four for an SMG or auto pistol) is how many rounds a disciplined tap fires: a bot's tap rhythm and the Disciplined medal. A set-down bipod is the one thing that tames a held machine gun: it still blooms, 1.4 to 1.8 times by round 30, against 1.9 to 2.8 times stood up without it. A scoped gun's kick, cap and decay are scaled by one shared factor (the sight picture is lost and found, not just the muzzle moved) and a bolt-action's further by another (working the bolt breaks the cheek weld): that is where a sniper's big per-shot bloom comes from. Walk speed falls by the same share for every kilogram carried (gun, plus a little for its length, plus armor), never under the 58% floor, and the share of the sprint bonus you keep falls with the square of the load. What stays written per gun is what its role needs and physics does not give: bursts, the bipod, the rev-up, falloff, the shotgun's pellet pattern and the gun's base spread, plus explicit, marked `overrides` on a gun (none are needed today) and two class exceptions, both the shotgun's: every shotgun settles off a sprint in 0.6 of what its build gives (`HANDLING.shotgunSettleMul`; a pump gun about 1.3 s, not 2.1), because coming off a sprint and blasting is its job, and its bloom is held to 0.65 of its build (`HANDLING.shotgunCapMul`), because its pellet pattern is its spread. **People bloom less than bots.** Everything above is a gun's own bloom, which is what a bot gets. A person gets half the bloom cap (the most a spray widens past the gun's own cone), half the kick a round adds and twice the recovery (half the `recoverMs`) of a bot on the same gun (`HUMAN_BLOOM` and `bloomFor` in `src/shared/defs.ts`, the one place bloom is read for a shooter). It is a deliberate edge so people can outfight bots, the one stat where they differ. It applies in every mode: FFA, TDM, Domination, Last Standing, Zombies and the range. The rounds to the cap are unchanged, so every round of a person's spray blooms exactly half what a bot's does and a full spray clears in half the time. An assault rifle held down on the move caps at 9.1 degrees either side for a person against 14.9 for a bot, an SMG 8.2 against 10.1, an LMG 13.4 against 20.2, and a bolt-action's follow-up kick is 5.8 degrees against 11.7. Falling bloom still eases out over `spreadEaseDownMs` (the person's own `recoverMs`, never under 100 ms), so a person's reticle never snaps either. Steady Hands, a bipod and a scope's bloom compose with it as they do for a bot. Whether a gun holds its heat until it could fire again (`coolsAfter`) is decided on the gun's own recovery, so a person's quicker recovery never makes a fast-fired gun bloom more. The page predicts a person's bloom (`src/client/fire.ts`), and a bot brain reads its seat's own bloom (`Rhythm.kind` in `motor.ts`). The tables print the gun's own numbers, a bot's.

**View.** With no bonus you see 700 px to either side (`WORLD.viewRadius`, zoomed in about 10% from 780 so bodies and rounds read bigger; the height follows the screen's shape, so 16:9 sees 700 by 394 px each way from you, and the stronger aim look-ahead gives back the ground down the aim). Two things widen it: the gun's scope (`viewMul`: +20 to +36% on every sniper, the Scout +12%, the Marksman +7%, the Executioner, Rail Slug, Battle Rifle and Grenadier +5%), and Optics (+12%, an attachment for pistols and assault rifles). They stack with diminishing returns up to a cap for the class (`VIEW` in `src/shared/defs.ts`, `viewMulFor` in `src/shared/sim/stats.ts`): each bonus closes its share of the gap still left to the cap, so one alone is worth all of itself, the next less, and the total never reaches 1.45 times the base for a sniper or 1.18 for any other class. The widest view in play is a Piercer's at 1.36 times (952 px); a Scout with Optics sees 1.16 times (812 px). The camera also leans toward your aim (below). The camera, the server's snapshot culling (with its aim look-ahead widening) and bot sight all use that one view radius, so a bot sees no further than a human with the same kit. Bots see by what a person's screen shows (`botSight` in `src/server/bot/awareness.ts`): the typical 16:9 screen (`BOT_VIEW_ASPECT` in `src/shared/protocol.ts`), 700 by 394 px each way with no bonus, widened toward where the bot aims by the full lean a person's camera gets (`lookReach`, below), with the same scope and Optics bonuses and caps. So a scoped sniper bot sees as far down its aim as a scoped person (about 1520 px for a plain Bolt-action, 1620 for a Piercer) but only about 500 to 550 px up and down beside it and its view radius behind it, and has to turn to see the rest; it used to see its whole view radius every way (896 to 977 px). Its snapshot is the interest a person with its gun, perks and aim is sent (`botSnapshot` in `src/server/bot/tick.ts`), and a test holds the two boxes equal, so a bot spots you when your screen could have spotted it.

**Aim look-ahead.** The camera leans toward where you aim, further the nearer the cursor is to the screen's edge (full at 80% of the way there, in any direction): by 36% of your view radius for a shotgun, 39% for an SMG, 45% for a pistol, 48% for an LMG, 50% for an assault rifle and 70% for a sniper (`LOOK_AHEAD.share`), so aimed at the side edge of a 16:9 screen an assault rifle shows 350 px more ground that way (1050 px down the aim, about what the wider view showed before the zoom) and a scoped sniper about 630 (1520 px). A gun that reaches past the edge of its view (every sniper, the long rifles, slugs and machine guns) leans at least far enough that its full lean shows its whole range, up to 75% of the view (`lookReach` in `src/shared/lookahead.ts`). However far it leans, your own soldier stays at least 15% of the screen from every edge, on any shape of screen (an ellipse at 70% of what is on screen, `boundLean` in `src/client/camera.ts`, wide enough that a scoped sniper leans in full down the long side of a 16:9 screen), so a lean toward a short side (up and down on a wide screen, sideways on a phone held upright) stops sooner. A thumb on the aim stick leans 70% as far and keeps the soldier a quarter of the screen from the edges. The lean eases in and out (an exponential follow, a little quicker while firing or planted) and snaps only on a respawn or teleport. Settings, Aim look-ahead: Normal, Low (0.3 of it, about the first, subtler lean) or Off; reduced motion halves it.

**Minimap and hearing.** The minimap shows your teammates, the objectives, the supply plane and drops, and an enemy only when something marks them: a hunted player's ping (see above), a Tracker mark (an enemy you hurt), a Recon mark (an enemy who hurt you), a radar tag or a Thermal blip; a Ninja shows only on the hunted ping. Firing does not put anyone on it, silenced or not. You hear gunfire instead: another soldier's shot plays in stereo where it was fired, fading out at 1.2 times your view radius, and a silenced one is a muffled thup. Bots have no stereo, so the server gives each one a hearing cue (`BOT_HEARING` in `src/shared/sim/hearing.ts`, carried in a bot's own snapshot as `heard`, never on the wire): an enemy's unsilenced shot within 2.4 times the base view radius (1680 px), twice a person's earshot on purpose to keep bots competitive (a sniper or LMG carries 25% further, 2100 px, a shotgun 10%), a silenced shot only within 350 px, each placed only roughly, off by up to 20% of its distance. Earshot has the screen's shape (`earDist`): those reaches are to either side, and only 1/1.78 of them above or below (945 px for an ordinary shot), so a bot hears no further up or down than a person's 16:9 screen shows against its width; the enemies it only remembers when it counts those about it to weigh a fight (`othersOn`, 750 px to either side, 422 up or down) are measured the same way. A bot's own scope and Optics widen its eyes, not its ears (a fully scoped sniper bot used to hear 2345 px, 2931 for a loud gun, half the map), and a shot it placed only thanks to the loud bonus, past 1680 px, is somewhere to go and look, never a spot it points its gun at (`aimsAtLead`). A bot with nothing in sight goes to search toward what it heard, the nearest hunted ping or Tracker mark first; a flashed bot hears nothing.

**Sprint.** Hold Shift (on a phone, push the move stick out to the outer ring that appears beyond its walking ring) while moving to sprint: you move up to 1.65 times faster, applied after the 58% loadout floor so Lightweight stacks with it, and how much of that bonus you get depends on your load (`sprintShareOf`: a light load gets a little more than all of it, an assault rifle about all, an LMG about 60%, a Minigun, a Juggernaut or anything in heavy armor hardly any). A pistol sprints at about 455 px/s and crosses a 6000 px map edge to edge in about 13 s, against 23 s walking; the heaviest loadout sprints at about 158 px/s. The gun is lowered while you sprint: it cannot fire, though you can still reload and use abilities. A click ends the sprint (the held key no longer counts) and fires on the same tick: there is no wait for the gun. What a sprint costs is **bloom**. The moment it ends your spread is about 4 times the gun's moving spread (`SPRINT.settleMul`, wider than any walk) and it eases out (a squared curve, so most of it is gone by the halfway point) to whatever your stance gives (your walking spread if you keep moving, your standing or planted spread if you stop) over the gun's settle time (`settleRulesOf`; Marathon halves it, Steady Hands takes a quarter off). Settling fast off a sprint is a light gun's edge, and it comes from the gun's weight and length (`settleMs` in Handling): about 0.3 s for a pistol, a Gunslinger or a Skirmisher, 0.5 s for an SMG, 1.4 s for a Carbine, 1.7 s for a Heavy SMG, 2 s for an assault rifle or a shotgun, and 2.5 s for a sniper, an LMG and the heaviest guns. It breaks a planted sniper's pinpoint while it lasts; suppression and a shot's own bloom stack on top. **Spread never jumps**: every change of it (walking to standing, a bipod setting down, a sprint starting, suppression, a perk, a gun swap) eases in over at least 250 ms (`SPREAD_EASE`; the sim keeps the last nine ticks' spread targets and a shot gets their mean, `easeSpread`), and only a shot's own bloom kick lands at once. Bloom comes back down at the gun's own pace, never at once: the sim eases the spread without bloom and the bloom on top separately, and once the bloom is falling it ramps out over the gun's `recoverMs` kept between 100 and 250 ms (`spreadEaseDownMs`; 100 ms for an SMG or a pistol, about 125 for an assault rifle, the full 250 for an LMG, a Minigun or a sniper), while any rise, and any change of the spread under the bloom, still takes the full 250 ms. Your reticle draws that same eased spread: the client steps it tick by tick from your inputs exactly as the server does (`spreadOf` in `src/client/fire.ts`), so it opens wide while you sprint, is wide the moment you stop and visibly tightens as the bloom settles. The sprint state and the post-sprint bloom ride in your own snapshot (`sprint`, `sprintSpeed`, `settle`, `settleMs`; `settle` is the share of the bloom still to ease out) so prediction of your movement, your shots and your reticle match the server. You and others see a sprinting soldier with the gun carried across the chest, a longer stride, the head leaning in, bigger dust puffs and faint speed streaks; when the sprint ends the gun swings back up to the aim in a short, purely cosmetic swing (`swingMsOf`, from the gun's build: 0.15 s for a pistol up to 0.28 s for the heaviest), overshooting a hair as it locks on. Bots sprint only to travel (after 1.5 s out of any fight) with no enemy in sight or to run to cover; they walk the moment they see one, so they can fire, and a tapping bot lets the post-sprint bloom settle before a long shot as it does its own spray bloom. Shift+M still toggles the music.

**Picks in every mode.** Every pick does something in every mode. Most work the same everywhere; those built for fights between players (sight, sound, minimap marks, armor) have a Zombies job of their own that keeps their theme, and in a zombies run the pick panel and the loadout tile show that line instead (`PERK_INFO.zom` in `src/shared/defs.ts`, the rules in `PERK_RULES` in `src/shared/sim/stats.ts` and `src/shared/sim/zomperks.ts`). No two picks share a job: Optics is the one view pick, Recon is intel about who shoots you, Quick reload is the pure reload pick while Fast hands pays out on kills.

Ammo is unlimited: a reload always refills the magazine, so no perk or pickup touches a reserve.

A kill refuels the killer on the spot: 35% of their max health back and half a mag (not mid-reload), so winning a fight carries you into the next instead of sending you off to wait out the regen delay. Kills in one life make a streak. From three kills a flame and the count show beside your name for everyone, and ending a streak of five or more pays a 100-point shutdown bonus. Whoever killed you last is your nemesis, marked over their head when you see them; killing them back pays 50 for revenge and settles it. Kills within 3 seconds of each other chain into double, triple and quad kills, and streak milestones are named at 3, 5, 8, 12 and 20. The kill sting climbs two semitones a kill up the streak, and a kill punches the camera and rings out from the crosshair. The death card sums up the life you lost (kills, damage, level and time survived), stamps each stat that beat this browser's record, and names your new nemesis. Its loadout re-pick and Respawn sit under the recap; the card never grows past the screen (its body scrolls inside it and the button row stays put), Respawn counts down the timer on its face, and Space respawns with the same loadout once it is up. On a phone on its side the card goes in steps and shows only what matters (`src/client/deathflow.ts`): first who killed you and with what, one gold line for the life's most notable number (a new best, or kills, damage and time alive), a small More for the stat tiles, records and nemesis, and one Try again button; then the loadout as three lean rows of touch-sized chips (weapon, color, armor) with Respawn always in view under them. While you are dead the touch controls (reload, the ability, GG, the radio button and the sticks) fold away, so nothing looks tappable that is not.

Medals are a bonus on top of the kill, not most of the score. Each is set so it lands on a minority of kills: the everyday ones (Double Kill, On Fire, Clutch, Point Blank, Long Shot and each class's common feat) on at most about one kill in five of their kind, the rare ones on a few in a hundred. In rooms of bots medals pay about a fifth of a life's score, kills three fifths and assists the rest. `node scripts/level-scale.ts` prints how often each medal fires, the share of score it pays and the score a life has after each kill, which is what the ladder above is set on. Zombie kills pay 1.6 times their score, since a run pays no medals. The moment you earn one it punches onto the screen above centre in the manner of Call of Duty: a struck metal medal (`src/client/medals.ts`, one SVG per medal on a bronze, silver, gold or platinum blank with ribbon, laurel or wings) flashes, a shine sweeps it, its name and score land under it with a bell for its metal, and medals earned together line up side by side.
