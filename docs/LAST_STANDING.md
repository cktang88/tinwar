# Last Standing

**Friends.** Click a person's name on the scoreboard and pick Add friend; they get an invite plate to accept or decline (it lapses after 20 s). A bot never accepts (the invite reads as sent, as any does, and simply lapses unanswered), and a friendship lasts while you are both in the room. The friend menu also links to the player's service record. Friends cannot hurt each other by any means, always show on each other's minimap, and in TDM and DOM play on one team (whoever sent the invite crosses over). The moment an invite is accepted, whoever sent it is brought straight to their new friend's side, in every mode, and a respawn (or a Last Standing redeploy) lands beside a standing friend.

**Cheats.** Two chat codes, checked on the server and never sent to the page: one gives you the score to your next level pick at once, the other toggles bots dealing you half damage for the rest of your stay in the room. A code is never shown in chat; only its typist is told what it did. The codes are set by `TINWAR_CHEAT_LEVELUP` and `TINWAR_CHEAT_EASY` on the server (see `src/server/cheats.ts` for the defaults), so a deployment can keep its own secret.

Last Standing, the BR room, is a battle royale where every player is on their own: eighteen of them, people and bots, everyone an enemy except your friends (see Friends). A match plays one of the versus maps, the next map in the rotation each time, and everyone drops in spread out, as far from each other as the map allows.

**You start light.** Every life starts with your class gun at level 1 and **no armor**, whatever your loadout says. Power is out on the map:

- **Loot caches.** Forty-eight small crates are strewn over the map, at least 300 px apart, each glowing the colour of its tier so you can see from across the screen what is worth the walk. Stand at one for 5 s to open it, with a ring of its colour filling round it and the seconds left above, so you are a target while you do (step away and it starts over; whoever got there first has it). A **Common** (grey) one pays 90 score toward your next level pick and refills your magazine. A **Rare** (blue) one pays 220 score, heals 45 and puts your armor up a tier (none, light, medium, heavy). An **Epic** (purple) one skips you straight to your next pick, puts your armor up a tier and resupplies you in full. Opened caches stay on the map, open and empty, so you can see where someone has been. About three in ten are **weapon cases**, long and low with a rifle stencilled on the lid: opening one leaves a gun of its tier on the floor, a class gun from a common case, a first evolution from a rare one and a final evolution (rarest of all, about one a map) from an epic one. Stand over a gun on the floor and press **E** to take it with a full magazine; the gun you had lies where it was, for anyone else. An attachment the new gun cannot use comes off, and your next evolve pick follows the new gun. The dead drop their gun and an armor plate, so every kill pays: walk over the plate to put one plate (16 points) back in a worn vest, never past full and nothing without a vest, and press E for the gun (the TAKE button on a phone; one gun per press, so holding E never swaps back). Holding a final evolution makes you hunted, as in the other modes: it pings your place on enemy minimaps. Guns on the floor glow their stage's colour and name themselves when you are close, with `[E] TAKE` once you are in reach. Phones have no E, so on a phone you keep the gun you have. Rare and epic caches show on the minimap until they are opened. `LOOT` in `src/shared/defs.ts`.
- **Recon towers.** Three antenna masts stand far apart. Stand inside a ready tower's circle alone for 2.5 s (a second player inside, or stepping out, starts it over) and every other player within 2200 px shows on your minimap for 20 seconds. Then the tower rests for 60 seconds, counting down on its mast. They are loud, exposed places to stand, which is the point. `TOWER` in `src/shared/defs.ts`.
- **Supply drops.** Each ring phase drops one 20 seconds into its wait, on open ground inside the next circle, shown on every minimap with a countdown from 10 seconds before it lands. Breaking it gives what every supply drop gives (see [Airdrops](ARENA.md)): a skip to your next level pick and a full resupply, or the golden gun with every pick made.
- **Kills** pay score as everywhere else, so fighting levels you too.

The whole map is safe for the first 60 seconds. Then the safe circle closes in six phases, each new circle inside the last and centred on open ground: to 3200 px over 30 seconds, then after 45 seconds to 2500 px over 25, after 40 seconds to 1900 px over 20, after 30 seconds to 1300 px over 20, after 25 seconds to 700 px over 15, and after 20 seconds to nothing over 15. Outside the circle a player loses 2% of their max health a second in the first phase, then 3, 5, 8, 12 and 20%, as a pulse of half that every half second on the world clock. The ring burns through armor and the spawn shield, and nobody outside it regenerates. `RING` in `src/shared/defs.ts` lists the phases.

There are no knocks: a player whose health runs out is dead. Until the second phase closes, a dead player redeploys after 12 seconds (8 seconds longer for each earlier death in the match) at a spot inside the circle far from everyone, with their class gun, no armor, back at level 1, and a spawn shield; the HUD then announces Last lives, and a death after that is out for good. A dead player's camera follows whoever killed them, then anyone left in the match.

The last one standing wins. Players who go out in the same tick place by their kills, more kills placing higher. The HUD shows how many are left (`12 / 18 left`) and your kills; the kill feed says who is out and in what place, and your result card reads `#1 · Last one standing` or `#7 of 18`, with your kills and the caches you looted. The next match starts 8 seconds after the win. A person who joins while redeploys are open takes the place of a bot still in the match, where it stood; one who leaves hands their place back to a bot. After that a joiner watches until the next match takes them in.

Bots play the versus brain on their own. With no fight on they run errands: some go for a supply drop in reach, some take a ready tower near them and hold it, and the rest loot, walking to the best unopened cache inside the next circle (an epic is worth a longer walk than a common) and on to the next. They head for the next circle in time, walk back in when the ring catches them, fight what they see without chasing across the ring, and reload or heal in cover between fights (`src/server/bot/royale.ts`; `node scripts/bench-royale.ts` plays whole matches).
