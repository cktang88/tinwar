# Music and radios

The soundtrack is played live in the browser in one adaptive framework: a calm layer (always), a combat layer that swells with the fight, a hype layer (on a streak, a multi-kill or being hunted), a finale layer (the last 60 seconds, or a boss) and, in the Zombies night, a heartbeat layer. Drums, synth leads and basses are synthesized (oscillators, filtered noise, envelopes; among them an 808 that glides, a phonk cowbell, a synth pluck, an eight-bit triangle bass, a riser and an impact for the drops); the acoustic and electric instruments are short samples from the Fluid R3 General MIDI soundfont (MIT licence, notice in `public/music/LICENSE.txt`), a few notes each as small mono MP3s in `public/music/` (`node scripts/build-music-samples.ts` rebuilds them). A track loads only its own samples when it starts, and its synth stand-ins play until they arrive (`musicsamples.ts`). Kills ring a sting built on the chord that is playing, so it is always in key, and a round's end plays a win or loss cadence on the track's tonic. **Shift+M** turns the music off and on; **M** mutes everything. The map id the server sends picks the track, and a map change crossfades to the new map's track over 3.5 s at a bar line (`music.ts`: two decks, equal-power curves). The menu and the Plaza play the original Toy March (`musicclassic.ts`, restored from the first soundtrack).

Every other map plays its own synthesized theme, written to a few pop-songwriting rules (`docs/music/CRAFT.md`): one short, rhythmic hook that keeps coming back, one proven four-chord loop, and an arc that builds in layers. An 8-bar intro (beat and pad, then the bass with the hook teased) leads into a 32-bar loop of four sections: the hook, a call-and-response groove (the lead jumps an octave in a fight), a breakdown and build (a riser, then a silent beat), and the drop (an impact, the big bass and the hook; half-time on an 808 for the trap and phonk themes). After the drop the loop comes round to the hook. Something changes every 8 bars (fills, octave jumps, the counter-melody, the comping), and every other time round the drop doubles the hook. The calm layer is a small, complete version of the song with the hook always in it; a fight adds the backbeat, the full kit and the lead; a streak adds the counter-melody; the finale adds sixteenth hats and a harmony. A theme is a short `PopSpec` (`musicthemes.ts`: the hook and counter-melody as notes, bass riffs as chord degrees, the band and the beat as patterns), arranged bar by bar by `musicpop.ts`; nothing about the tune depends on the seed, which only picks fills.

Night Market keeps Eric Skiff's "We're All Under the Stars", which players asked for, the one recording left. It streams from `public/music/tracks/` (`musicstream.ts`), opening up from a muffled low-pass when calm to full range in a fight, with a few seconds of the drop from "Wraghstep [v2]" (a 5 s sting, `drop.mp3`) over it on a five-kill streak; while the file loads, or if it fails, its synthesized theme stands in. Every recording is credited in `public/music/tracks/CREDITS.md` (`node scripts/music-credits.ts` writes it from the registry) and in the pause menu. `node scripts/render-music.ts <outDir>` renders each track offline through the real music rig and master bus (`scripts/musicrender.ts` also has `renderSample`, a 45 s listening sample from calm into a fight); headless Chrome's `OfflineAudioContext` is the faithful loudness measure (its compressor is the players'), and the themes are trimmed to about -14 LUFS in a fight.

**Radios.** The range and the Zombies outpost each have one chunky toy radio (by the range booth, and beside the Bastion core): a warm light, a speaker that thumps on the beat, a moving dial and music notes drifting up. Walk up and an **E** keycap prompt shows "Radio - station" (on a phone, a Radio button appears); E cycles through all fifteen stations (one per track) and Off (the first press goes to the first track) the way a real radio does: the old station cuts out within 50 ms under a quarter-second burst of static and the new one starts at once, mid-bar, on its hook. The client plays the new station as you press and sends `{ t: 'radio', station }` (presses faster than the server takes them are ignored on both sides, so you never drift from the squad); the server accepts it only in ZOM and RNG rooms, at most once per 350 ms per client, and tells everyone in the room (and a late joiner), so a squad hears one station. In the range the choice is saved in `localStorage` (`skirmish.radio.station`); a saved station that no longer exists is ignored, so the radio falls back to the map's own track. In Zombies a night keeps the Bastion's night score whatever the radio plays (its heartbeat and menace are the night's alarm) and the station returns at dawn; Off stays off. A round through a radio makes it sputter for a moment. Bots ignore radios, and a radio has no collision.

On every versus map (FFA, TDM, DOM, Last Standing) six to eight smaller **hidden radios** (at least 900 px apart where the map allows, never under 600) sit tucked into corners and alcoves, re-rolled every round: `scripts/radio-pool.ts` picks 30 spots per map (walkable and reachable from a spawn by the map-lint rasters, a wall within 100 px on a run of three to five compass directions and never on opposite sides, 220 px clear of spawn pads and zone-radius plus 160 px clear of zones) into `src/shared/radiopool.ts`, and each client picks from them with a generator seeded by the map and the round's name (`match.roundEndsAt`, or `royale.round` in Last Standing), so every player sees the same radios and the sim's random numbers are untouched. A hidden radio retunes only you (client only, no message): the tracks, "Map default" and Off, until the round ends. The first radio found in a session says "Found a radio!"; finds are counted (`radioFinds()`) for a possible future challenge, with no XP or medal yet.
