# Zombies

Zombies is a co-op mode for a private squad of up to four. Start a squad from the menu's Zombies block and send friends its invite link (`?squad=<code>`), which opens the menu with that squad selected. Under the hood `POST /api/squads` opens a squad room and answers `{ "room": "z-xxxxxx" }`; everyone who joins the WebSocket with `?room=<code>` plays in it. Squads stay off the public room list, one address may open six a minute, at most 20 run at once, and a squad closes 30 seconds after its last human leaves. Bots fill the empty seats and give a seat up when a human joins. Humans keep their fourfold health. The whole squad is one team, and nothing the squad does hurts the squad.

The squad defends the Bastion, a core at the center of the Outpost map with 50 survivors inside. A run opens on a 40-second day, which ends early once every living human presses N. Night follows, and a horde walks in toward the core from the map edges the night table names. Later nights send their packs in waves: night N sends N-1 packs at once, then waits as long as those packs would have taken one at a time. By day the HUD forecasts tonight with the deadliest kinds first and the horde's size for your squad, for example `Tonight · Brutes, walkers and runners from the east · 37 strong`. The night ends once the whole horde has spawned and died. First light comes 90 seconds after the last pack walks in and burns whatever is still out for no scrap, so a zombie that cannot reach anything never holds the night; the phase line counts down to it. `NIGHTS` in `src/shared/defs.ts` lists ten nights, one row each, with each kind's count for a squad of four bots and its sides. Night 1 is 24 walkers and a brute from the north; night 2 brings seven brutes, which ignore the squad and march on the core, and from night 3 the horde splits, two or three sides at once and later all four, so a squad standing in one spot cannot hold it and turrets have to cover the sides it is not on. Night 5 brings a Colossus, night 8 another, and night 10, the Tide, brings two and every kind from every side. Each night after the first a zombie has 22% more health than on night 1, bites 12% harder and walks 2.5% faster (to 20% faster by night 9), `ZOM.nightMul`. A human counts as one and a half bots toward the horde's size. A boss, a kind that walks alone like the Colossus, comes once for any squad and has its health scaled by the squad's share instead. Holding through the Tide is the run's great moment, `THE TIDE HELD`, and it pays Held the Line, but the run goes on: only the core's fall ends a run. Past the table the nights are endless (`ENDLESS` and `nightOf` in `src/shared/defs.ts`), and the HUD reads `Night 12 · Endless`. Each is built from the Tide, every kind from every side, with each kind's count 8% bigger a night for ten nights past it and then held, and another Colossus every second night, up to six. Past that the horde grows only in health and bite, by `ZOM.nightMul`, and never more than 200 walk at once (`ZOM.maxAlive`): a wave brings only the packs that fit under that cap, and the rest wait for room. Losing the last survivor ends nothing either. The fallen are then not sent back at night and wait for dawn, which stands every squad player up free, while the core fights on with its turrets until first light. When the core falls the run is over: `The Bastion fell on night N`, or, for a squad that held the Tide on the way, `The Tide held. The Bastion fell on night N, M past the Tide.` A report of the night reached, the run's length, each player's kills, revives and buildings put up, and the kills of the turrets and the Bastion shows for 20 seconds, and then a fresh run starts on a reset map.

Every 100 hp of harm the core takes costs one survivor, however well the core is mended between bites, so repairing the core keeps it standing but raises no one. At night the HUD shows tonight's dead beside the survivor count. At dawn each survivor left pays 3 scrap. The survivors also shoot from the core's walls at zombies within 220 px, slower as they die.

A bite on a squad player takes 60% of what the same bite takes off a wall or the core, and never more than a third of a player's health however late the night (`ZOM.playerBite`), so nothing kills from full health in fewer than three bites. A walker bites a squad player it can see within 160 px. Runners are small and fast, come in packs and chase the squad from 360 px. Plated walkers shrug 10 damage off every bullet, so small rounds barely scratch them while heavy rounds, piercing rounds and blasts get through. A bloater bursts where it dies, hurting the squad, the horde, every building within 110 px and the core if it is that close, so it is best killed far off. A brute and the Colossus, a far bigger brute, ignore the squad and march on the core. Otherwise a zombie bites the core once it reaches it, and the core shrugs off 40% of each bite. On the way it follows a flow field to the core that goes round the squad's walls while there is an open way and breaks through the cheapest wall when there is not. Zombie kills pay score up the same level ladder as the versus modes, and each kill adds scrap to the squad's shared bank: 3 for a walker, 2 for a runner, 7 for a plated walker, 6 for a bloater, 15 for a brute and 80 for a Colossus, on top of the 140 a run opens with. Scrap only buys buildings, upgrades, repairs and rounds, so a squad that never builds banks it for nothing. A player's level, perks and gun last for the whole run.

Every pick has a job against the horde too: the picks built for versus (Thermal, Ghillie suit, Silencer, Shield, Adrenaline, Bloodlust, Recon, Ninja, Tracker, Brace, Knife, Shield wall and Radar) do something of their own in a run, and the pick panel shows that line (see Picks in every mode, under Play).

Every gun has a job against the horde, in line with its job in versus (`ZOMBIE_CLASS_ROLES` and `zombieRole` in `src/shared/defs.ts`, applied in `src/shared/sim/zomroles.ts`; none of it reaches a versus room or a player). A player's own kill pays the zombie's scrap times the bounty of the gun in their hand, `zombieBounty`, and the spread is stark. It reads each gun's sustained harm through a night of fire, `sustainedHarm`: a round's harm at 300 px (falloff applied, every pellet counted, a blast on two) times the magazine, over the time to empty it at full rate and reload. A class pays 106.25 (`BOUNTY.par`, the assault rifle's sustained harm at its old 1.5 s reload, kept fixed so a reload change does not reprice every other gun) over its class gun's sustained harm, to the power 2.25, held between 0.5 and 3; an evolution pays its class's bounty times the square root of the class gun's harm over its own, to the 0.05. The LMG's 100-round belt out-harms everything else through a night (136 a second against the assault rifle's 96, the Minigun 254), so the machine guns pay 0.5 to 0.6 of the scrap a kill, the assault rifles 1 to 1.3, the SMGs 1.2 to 1.8, the pistols 1.2 to 1.5, the shotguns 1.65 to 2.65 and the snipers 1.7 to 3 (the bolt-action 3). A turret's kill and the Bastion's pay the plain price, and each `zkill` event carries what the kill paid. On top of that:

| Class | Against the horde |
| --- | --- |

No gun pierces zombies differently from players: a round passes through as many zombies as its gun's own `penetrate` lets it pass through players, in every mode. A few evolutions do their own job: the Rail Slug hits the heavies 1.4 times as hard, the Executioner 1.3 times, and the Boom Slug, Grenadier and Artillery blast the horde 1.3 times as hard but a plated walker or the Colossus takes only half of a blast. The strongest hold on a zombie wins, and brutes and the Colossus, which take no shove, shrug off holds too. With the guns this much stronger against the horde, a player's gun deals the horde 66% of its harm (`HORDE_GUN_MUL`, before the multipliers above; turrets keep all of theirs), which keeps a squad that never builds falling on night 3 while a building squad holds as long as before. In Zombies the gear step's gun tiles, the evolve pick and the HUD's loadout strip (where the gun's tile shows from the start, with its bounty as a chip such as `×3` or `×0.55`) say each gun's bounty, in scrap a kill and per walker ("×3 scrap a kill (9 per walker)"), and its job.

By day a player presses B for build mode (a pistol-class gun's holder by night too, by the same rules and prices; anyone else is told to build by day) and puts up a building on a 50 px grid cell within 600 px of the core and 250 px of themselves, on a cell clear of cover, the core, bodies and other buildings. A cell refused only because someone stands on it still sends the build: the server notes the cell as wanted for 5 s (`Run.wanted`), a squad bot standing on it steps off within a few ticks, and the next click builds. Squad bots also never take their post on a wanted cell or on the cell the squad's build plan puts up next, so a bot never stands for good where the next building should go. The build bar lists what can go up in three rows, walls by tier, turrets and utilities, and the keys 1 to 9, 0 and -, or a click or tap on a chip, pick it: 1 the wall, 2 a sentry (70 scrap), 3 a cannon (120), 4 a scatter (100), 5 a mortar (140), 6 a tesla coil (150), 7 a salvage yard (150), 8 a medic post (100), 9 a spike strip (10), 0 a flame vent (100) and - a decoy beacon (50). Hovering a kind in build mode says its job in one line (`Scatter: shoves and slows packs`, `Mortar: long range, can't hit inside 300 px`, `Tesla: stuns and marks — +25% gun damage to marked`), and a standing one says it under its upgrade line. The wall comes in three tiers, picked on the bar's top row or by pressing 1 again, Q or the mouse wheel (which steps through everything): a barricade of boards for 10 scrap and 800 hp, a sandbag wall for 25 and 2400 hp (3× a barricade's), and a steel wall for 60 and 4800 hp (6×). Every wall stops a zombie outright; a sandbag wall takes 10% less damage from each bite and 0.75 of a bloater's burst, steel 20% less and half, and a barricade mends fastest (1.5× the usual rate, steel 0.8×). Each tier is the buy somewhere: a barricade is the cheapest cell to fill and the quickest to mend, a sandbag wall soaks up the most bite a scrap, and a steel wall holds the most in one cell and the most burst a scrap. With a wall or a spike strip picked, pressing on a cell and dragging lays a line of them: it runs straight from that cell along whichever way the drag went farther, across or up and down, up to 12 cells, and the ghost shows every cell with its price, green where it will go up and red where it cannot (out of reach, blocked, taken, or past what the bank can pay), with the line's count and total on its plate. Letting go builds it in one message (`{ t: 'build', kind, cells }`), the server putting each cell up in order from where the drag began by the same rules as a single build, so the line stands as far as the scrap lasts and a cell that cannot take it is passed over; Esc or a right click drops the line, and a press let go where it began builds the one. Build mode needs a keyboard: on a phone there is no B, so phones cannot build. A building can come down by day (or by night with a pistol) for half of everything put into it back, less the share of it that is worn. Buildings block bodies, zombies included, but the squad's bullets and grenades pass over them, and a spike strip, which lies flat, blocks nobody. A sentry has 1000 hp, a scatter and a cannon 1100, a tesla coil and a salvage yard 900, a medic post 800, a flame vent 700, a mortar 600, a decoy beacon 1600 and a strip 450. A walker bites a building for half its damage and a brute for its full damage.

By day (or by night with a pistol) anything but a spike strip can be upgraded where it stands, up to level 3, for scrap: U upgrades the building under the cursor in build mode (or, outside it, the nearest one within 250 px, with a gold hint naming the step and its price), so does the build bar's upgrade chip, and so does a left click on a standing building that can step up. Hovering one in build mode shows its name, level, health, the next step's price and what taking it down pays. A wall steps from barricade to sandbag to steel for the difference of their prices (15, then 35). Anything else pays its build price again for each level, so a cannon is 120, then 120 for level 2 and 120 for level 3, and a level 3 building stands for three of them. Against level 1, a turret's level 2 and 3 do 1.5× and 2× the damage, fire 1.25× and 1.5× as often, reach 10% and 20% farther and hold 1.5× and 2× the load (a mortar's burst grows with its damage and its reach; a scatter's pellets do not hit harder but grip and shove 1.5× and 2× as hard; a tesla coil's arc leaps to two and four more zombies and marks 1.5× and 2× as long; a flame vent burns 1.5× and 2× as hot and its jet reaches 10% and 20% farther); every building has 2× and 3× the health, in step with the scrap put in, so mending costs the same a point at every level; a salvage yard pays +75% and +100% and reaches 25% and 50% farther, a medic post heals, revives and mends 1.5× and 2× as fast and reaches 25% and 50% farther, and a decoy draws the horde 25% and 50% farther. The hover's second line says what the next level is in those terms, e.g. `Cannon II: 1.5× dmg · 1.25× rate · +10% range · 1.5× ammo · 2× hp`, `Salvage yard II: +75% scrap · +25% reach · 2× hp`, `Medic post II: 1.5× heal · 1.5× revive · +25% reach · 2× hp` or `Steel wall: 6× hp · takes 20% less bite damage`.

Each turret has its own job rather than its own stat line (`BUILDINGS` in `src/shared/defs.ts`), so no price or damage tweak flips which one wins everywhere:

`node scripts/bench-zombie-builds.ts [seeds] [quick]` measures what a scrap buys: each buildable at each level in a scripted lane (`scripts/lib/zombiebuilds.ts`: the core walled in, the buildable just inside the south face, packs of the real horde streaming in) and prints, per turret and level, the horde health it takes off a scrap over three nights with its mending counted (ammo is free), what a step up adds a scrap beside a second level-I copy, its value against packs of each kind, each turret's job (the walk its holds and stuns cost the horde, the zombie-seconds it keeps marked, the harm it does far out, through a one-cell gap in the wall, and what it spares a steel wall), and the walls', spikes', salvage yard's (the extra scrap its kills pay and how many nights it takes to pay back), medic post's and decoy's worth. `test/zombies-buildvalue.test.ts` holds that no buildable is dominated on every axis (value a scrap against each kind, the heavies and the mix, range, far harm, pierce, time bought, wall saved, the squad's gun boost, chokepoint harm, pull, price, health a scrap and in one cell, burst health, mending speed, harm a scrap of mending, and what a utility alone does), that each wins its named job (the sentry `cheap`, the scatter `timeBought`, the cannon `vsHeavy` and `pierce`, the mortar `range` and `farHarm`, the coil `gunBoost`, the vent `chokepoint`, the salvage yard `scrapReturn`, the medic post `squadSustain`, the decoy `aggroPull`), and that each turret's steps up beat a second copy at that job, with a maxed turret under twice a fresh one's value a scrap. An upgrade keeps the building's share of health and load, and the turret's builder keeps the credit for its kills. A turret is a toy emplacement sandbagged onto the floor, its gun turning on a turntable to aim (`src/client/turretart.ts`), and it looks its level: steel plates bolted over the sandbags at level 2 and a riveted steel collar with hazard chevrons at level 3, more barrels (a sentry has two, three, then four under a perforated shroud, a scatter a fan of three, five, then seven, a mortar one, two, then three tubes), side shields, recoil cylinders, a muzzle brake's evacuator and a sighting optic, and gold pips in its corner. Its gun kicks back when it fires, with a flash at the barrel that fired and a warm pool on the floor; a lamp on its corner glows amber, blinks when the load runs low and red when dry, over a five-cell ammo gauge; and it shows its wear in soot, torn and missing sandbags and holed plates. A turret's range is a thin dashed ring of exactly the reach it aims within: round the build ghost before it goes up, round a built one under the cursor (in build mode, or by day outside it, else the one U would upgrade), with the next level's reach in dashed amber beside it by day. A sentry's, cannon's or scatter's ring is shaded where the map's walls and crates hide the floor from it, a mortar's (it lobs over cover) and a coil's (it arcs through it) are whole, and a mortar's has a red-hatched dead zone inside its 300 px minimum, where it cannot aim. A flame vent has no ring: its ghost shows the jet it would throw, washed in the ghost's colour with chevrons down it, facing straight out from the core until R turns it a quarter at a time (the facing, 0 to 3, goes up with the build message as `dir` and comes back on its building view); once built it is a squat steel base with a fuel tank and a nozzle along its facing, a scorch down its jet on the floor and, while it burns, a roaring tongue of fire with flickering licks, embers off its edges and a heat shimmer at its tip; a decoy beacon shows the reach it draws the horde from as a pulsing red ring in build mode. At night a faint dotted edge shows what each kind of the squad's turrets covers together (Settings, Turret ranges at night). Squad bots with no human in the squad build the ring of twelve turrets round the Bastion as before, but step the turret nearest the side tonight's horde comes from up to level 2 once nine stand, then add the last three turrets, a salvage yard and a medic post behind that side's guns, a line of four sandbag walls across its way (later steel), spike strips ahead of the line, a tesla coil beside it, and level 2 and 3 for every turret, the ones facing the horde first.

A turret fires real rounds at the nearest zombie in range that no map wall or crate hides, and the squad's own buildings never block its view. Each turret prefers one kind of zombie and fires at the nearest of that kind first. A turret whose round would lose more than half to plating leaves plated zombies and the Colossus to heavier guns. A sentry fires at walkers first, a scatter at runners, a cannon at brutes and then any heavy before the light kinds, a mortar at bloaters (lobbing its shell over map cover to where its target will be when it lands), and a coil at walkers. The scatter's holds and shoves, like a player's, pass brutes and the Colossus by. A held zombie bites as slowly as it walks, and a stunned one not at all. A tesla coil's bolt arcs from itself to its target and on to the nearest zombie within 120 px of each, through cover and plating alike and with no round to fly. A flame vent is solid like a turret (the horde bites it, nobody walks over it) and fires in bursts while a zombie is in its jet; its jet burns on whatever stands in it whether or not it has fuel, until the 1.2 s its last burst bought run out. An empty turret or a dry vent stops; its rounds and its fire never hurt the squad. A turret's kill (a vent's included) pays the bank its scrap and pays the turret's builder the score, but counts as the turret's kill, not the builder's.

Utilities work beside the guns. A salvage yard (`UTILITY.salvage`) is a scrap heap with a magnet crane swinging over it: every zombie killed within 225 px of it (280 and 340 at levels 2 and 3), by anyone or anything, pays 50% more scrap (75% and 100% at levels 2 and 3), and the yard pops a gold `+N` with a cog once a second for what it paid. Yards do not stack: a kill in reach of several pays the best one's bonus. It is an investment against defending now: at 150 scrap it pays itself back in two or three nights of fighting beside it in the early nights, and faster once the hordes are big. In build mode every yard, and one about to go up, shows its reach as a gold dashed ring with its bonus on it. A medic post (`UTILITY.post`) is a field tent with a green cross: it heals every squad player within 175 px 10 health a second, revives a downed one there by itself at 0.4 of a squadmate's pace (7.5 s, against 3 s by hand; a squadmate holding use is quicker and takes over), and mends every building there 8 a second, all free, day and night. While it works it gives off a soft mint pulse, and a downed squadmate it is reviving wears a mint revive ring with a turning dashed ring outside it and `MEDIC` under their clock. A spike strip is floor: zombies walk over it on a flow field that ignores it, slowed to 45% of their step and hurt 14 a second while they stand on it, and wear it down 7 hp a second each (24 for a brute, bloater or the Colossus) until it is gone. A decoy beacon (`UTILITY.decoy`) is a solid mast with a loudspeaker and a blinking red lamp: every zombie within 300 px of it (375 and 450 at levels 2 and 3) with no squad player to chase and a clear walk to it goes for the beacon instead of the walls and the Bastion and bites it until it falls, so a squad can pull a horde off a wall and into a kill zone. The Colossus pays it no mind. A tesla coil's mark shows on a zombie as a flickering blue ring at its feet and a bolt over its head, and a burning one carries its flames; each is a bit on the zombie's snapshot entry, sent only while something is on it. Holding use repairs the nearest damaged building or the core within 250 px at 80 hp a second, or reloads the nearest turret (or flame vent) short of ammo, day or night. A worn turret is repaired before it is reloaded, unless the bank is empty: reloading is free and mending is not. The `Hold E to ...` hint names the job by the same rule the server follows. Repairing a building costs half its price for the share of it that is worn, so mending always costs less than taking it down and building it again, and the core costs 1 scrap per 5 hp. Ammo is free: at each dawn every turret and flame vent is restocked to a full load (the `Turrets restocked` callout says how many), and at night a turret run dry is reloaded by hand, a full reload taking 2.5 seconds.

At dawn each squad player gets the night's receipt, printed out of a slot at the right edge like an old thermal till slip (`src/client/receipt.ts`): one line per source, sorted by damage, with its kills and damage, then a total, a thank-you and a barcode. A player is listed by name. A building is listed by its kind, with how many of that kind stand (`SENTRY ×3`), however many players built them. The Bastion's own fire, spike strips and stray blasts such as a bloater's burst each get a line too. The sim keeps the tally at the one place zombie damage lands (`damageZombie` in `src/shared/sim/run.ts`, keyed by `receiptKey`). It counts only the health a hit took, so overkill adds nothing, and a Tesla or Tracker mark's bonus counts for whoever dealt the hit. A new damaging building kind gets its own line with no extra code. The tally starts over when night falls, and dawn sends it once as a `receipt` event of `[source, damage, kills]` tuples. On a desktop the slip sits between the board and the minimap, and waits under the dawn XP card while that card shows. On a phone on its side it shows a two-line head, the top five lines and the total, under the top-right row and above the buttons, and it steps aside while the full board or the XP card is open. It stays for three quarters of the day, tears off when night falls, or tears off at once on a click or tap.

A player whose health runs out goes down instead of dying. A downed player crawls, cannot shoot and is ignored by the horde. A squadmate holding use within 70 px for 3 seconds revives them with 40% health. A downed player nobody revives within 25 seconds bleeds out. By night the Bastion sends them back 15 seconds later at the cost of survivors, 1 plus half the night's number rounded up, even the very last of them, and with too few survivors left they wait for dawn. A human who joins or rejoins during the night sits out until dawn. Bots fight, revive, keep a dry turret firing and post behind the squad's walls, finding their way round the core and the squad's buildings. In a squad with no human they also repair and reload while no zombie is close and put up a fixed ring of turrets by day (only by day: a bot holding a pistol does not build by night, which keeps the difficulty curve as tuned), the same distance out on every side, saving the scrap for each before they mend the core. With a human in the squad the bank is the humans': bots build nothing, keep enough for the dearest building, tend only with what is above that, and mend the core below it only when it falls under half at night.

The platinum Night Owl, Held the Line, Bricklayer and Titan Toppler unlock the Night Owl title, the First Light name colour, the Hard Hat helmet and the Titan Toppler title. Only a seated human's own play counts, signed in or a recorded guest, like every other career stat: bots keep no profile, the range records nothing and versus rooms add nothing to these tracks. The room reads what the run already says (`src/server/zomcareer.ts` and `payRun` and `creditProfiles` in `src/server/room.ts`): its kill, revive, shot and hit events, each accepted build and upgrade, the health each player mended (`Run.mended`, the one field the simulation keeps for it) and the core's harm. A night counts, toward Night Owl, Last Light and Not a Scratch, for whoever was seated from its dusk, the same rule as its XP. Rounds and mended health are paid to the profile in lumps of 25 and 250, the rest when the phase turns or the player leaves.
